# Synthea evaluation of Interim's deterministic text tools

> **Synthea synthetic EHR (simulated).** Every note, medication and vital sign scored here is Synthea output: synthetic patients, no real person. These numbers describe how Interim's rules behave on Synthea's templated notes. They are **not a clinical validation**.
>
> Generated by `scripts/synthea/eval_synthea.py`. Do not edit by hand. `eval_synthea.py --check` recomputes this file and requires it to be byte-identical.

## Why this evaluation matters

The chart-scan answer key (`packages/rules/data/chartscan_answer_key.json`, 25 cases) was written alongside the scanner's rules, so passing it is a **regression test, not an accuracy estimate**. Synthea's notes come from Synthea's own templates. The **medication and encounter** ground truth comes from Synthea's structured FHIR record, imported by `scripts/synthea/import_synthea.py`. The **symptom** ground truth is different: it is the note's own `# Chief Complaint` bullet list, which Synthea renders from its symptom model independently of the scanner, but which is the same text the scanner reads. So (a) mostly tests lexicon coverage on bullet text, not extraction from narrative. None of this ground truth was written by the scanner's author or by us. The Regeneron judges recommended Synthea, and the starter sheet names it as the source of chart-scan input. This is the first time the scanner meets text it was not tuned on.

## Method

- Each note is scanned once with `interim_rules.chartscan.scan_text(text, note_date)`, unchanged. Only kept (non-excluded) findings count as predictions.
- **Symptom ground truth** is the note's `# Chief Complaint` bullets, mapped to CTCAE v5.0 by `scripts/synthea/complaint_map.json`. Each complaint has its own rationale and the Synthea module that emits it. Complaints with no CTCAE term (thirst, hunger) are reported as out of scope, not as misses. Scoring is set-based per note at the concept level: a concept is a primary CTCAE term plus the alternatives accepted for it (chest pain may be coded cardiac, chest wall or pleuritic). Precision, recall and F1 are micro-averaged over notes.
- **Medication ground truth** per note is every medication active on the note date (structured MedicationRequest intervals plus the note's own `# Medications` list) plus the encounter's prescriptions, all from `notes.json`. A prediction is correct if it names any of them. Recall is reported two ways. **R(text)** covers only the items visible in the note text, which is all a text scanner can find. **R(all)** covers every item, including structured-only ones the text never mentions. Names are compared in lower case per ingredient; a salt suffix and the synonyms in `complaint_map.json` `ingredient_equivalents` count as equal.
- **Section-aware variant (a candidate improvement, not shipped):** split the `# Medications` line on `;` and run `interim_rules.conmed.match_conmed` on each item. This shows what a section-aware matcher would add. The scanner is not changed.
- **Escalation:** every red flag the scanner raises, broken down by the FHIR encounter class. Interim routes every chart-note flag to the coordinator as `urgent`; a note never pages anyone (D-017). Over-escalation is the share of ambulatory notes with any flag. Seriousness is checked against emergency and inpatient encounters.
- **Vitals:** Synthea's baseline pulse and breathing medians per participant, compared with the `vital` conditions read from `packages/rules/data/vitals_corroboration.json`.

The scanner's history exclusion only reaches 8 words (`chartscan_lexicon.json` `scope.negation_max_words`), so a past condition near the end of a long list is read as a current symptom. In Interim, the kiosk's hospitalization gate question is what starts the clock.
- Interim's note escalation levels: `none` 30, `urgent` 42. A chart note never pages anyone or stops a kiosk; a flag puts the note in the coordinator's urgent review queue.

## What this does and does not show

**Shows:**

- How the shipped scanner and con-med matcher behave on clinical text and medication names neither of them was tuned on. Medications and encounters are scored against Synthea's structured FHIR record; symptoms against Synthea's rendered chief-complaint list in the note (Synthea's own, independent of the scanner, but not structured).
- Concrete, reproducible failure modes, each with a quote and a note id: lexicon gaps, a history window too short for a long problem list, Levenshtein false matches on single words, and red flags fired by a templated complaint list.
- How much a cheap, deterministic, section-aware use of the existing matcher would add, measured before anyone builds it.

**Does not show:**

- **Clinical accuracy.** Synthea's notes are templated and far cleaner than real notes. There is no negation, no narrative, no abbreviations or typos, and every section repeats. On real notes, precision and recall would both be lower, and the scanner's negation logic is barely exercised here.
- **True symptoms.** Chief complaints are a proxy. Synthea renders the person's standing symptom list, not what was said at the visit. In this Synthea version the lung cancer module's dyspnea, fever and fatigue states all emit "Cough" (`complaint_map.json`), so the cohort shows no dyspnea at all.
- **Immune-related AEs.** Synthea treats NSCLC with cisplatin + paclitaxel and has no PD-1 inhibitor. These notes say nothing about the pneumonitis, colitis or rash that matter for cemiplimab.
- **Generalization.** 12 participants and 72 notes from one Synthea configuration. The counts are small and correlated, because notes repeat within a participant.
